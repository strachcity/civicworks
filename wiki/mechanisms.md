# Mechanisms

Claims made more than once across CIVICWORKS, 17 pieces, Sept 2025 to Oct 2026.

Working notes, not prose. Blunt on purpose: if an entry starts reading like an essay it has stopped being useful and become a draft.

Mined from the archive on 2026-08-20, piece 14 added 2026-08-25, X3 added 2026-09-17, pieces 15 to 17 added 2026-10-30. Piece numbers refer to publication order. Piece 03 is a guest post by Jack O'Connor and is excluded throughout.

Three pieces published outside CIVICWORKS are now included, and they change the picture:

- **T** = *(Re)imagining Mission Neighbourhoods: Emerging Practices of Urban Transformation.* MPA thesis, UCL IIPP. Submitted Sept 2025, published Feb 2026. Grounded theory, 15 interviews across the UK, Belgium, Canada and Australia. Supervised by Rainer Kattel and Kwame Baafi.
- **UN** = *How UN80 Can Reform the UN for a Volatile, Uncertain World.* IPI Global Observatory, Jun 2026. Uses "we" throughout; only one author bio appears in the saved page. Attribution needs confirming.
- **X3** = *Creating the conditions for transformation.* GDS blog, Sept 2026. Written in role at CustomerFirst, on the DVLA Drivers Medical partnership. Names three conditions from inside a live NewCo.

**X3 is a different genre and has to be read as one.** Everything else in the archive is a personal essay, a thesis or an op-ed, where the writer can say what he does not know. X3 is institutional writing in the first person plural, published by the organisation it describes. It can report conditions and it cannot report doubt. So what X3 says is usable, and what X3 omits proves nothing. Every entry below that cites it carries that discount.

**15 and 17 are the same case in his own name, and they lift part of that discount.** Both are essays about the CustomerFirst work with DVLA on Drivers Medical, the partnership X3 describes in role. They can say what X3 cannot: 15's "finding a skeleton doesn't necessarily mean you have the power to move it" is the doubt X3 had to leave out. Where an entry below needs the bind as well as the conditions, it now gets both from the same case.

**The other side of that: the live-case evidence is now mostly one partnership.** 12, 14's CustomerFirst observations, X3, 15 and 17 all come from it. Five sightings from one case are worth less than five from five, and M8 in particular rests almost entirely on it. See the gaps at the end.

**16 is an introduction, not an argument.** The one-year re-introduction. It is mined only for what it says is open, because it is the first time the author lists the corpus's open questions himself. They are four, and each one maps onto something already in this file: how learning should relate to authority (M1), how institutions build boundary-crossing into their operating models "rather than leaving particular people to glue everything together" (M5, prompt 06), what should be institutionalised and what should stay flexible (the institutionalisation paradox), and what happens when something can be built in hours while the institution moves in months (M8, prompt 04). The author's reading of the corpus and this file's reading agree.

## M1. Capability exists, and sits downstream of the decision

**The claim.** Government has spent 20 years building professions and methods for iterative learning, and positioned all of them after the moment direction is set, resources are committed and alternatives are foreclosed. The failure is not capability or intent. It is where the capability sits relative to decision authority.

This is the spine of the newsletter. Stated nine times about nine different subjects.

| Piece | Date | Subject | How it was put |
|---|---|---|---|
| 04 | Nov 25 | Missions | "governments are embracing the language of missions, they are implementing them through business-as-usual structures" |
| 06 | Jan 26 | Labs, challenge funds, hackathons | "Learning happens, but too late. Insight accumulates, but without leverage. Innovation is active, but powerless." |
| 09 | Mar 26 | Futures and foresight | "Futures is not absent from government. It is just not where decisions are made." |
| 10 | Apr 26 | Service design | "It arrives after the policy is set, after the commitments are made, after the assumptions have hardened." |
| 11 | May 26 | Innovation labs | "They produced excellent work and lost the argument about how decisions are made." |
| UN | Jun 26 | The United Nations | "the biggest decisions made up front when the least is known" |
| 14 | Aug 26 | Multidisciplinary teams | "Nobody is missing from the team, but the authority to follow the problem sits somewhere else" |
| 15 | Sept 26 | Discovery and the agile phases | "Government is classically very good at interrogating the risk of doing something, and infamously bad at making decisions" |
| 17 | Oct 26 | Experimentation | "Learning happens, but it arrives downstream of power" |

**15 gives M1 an economics, and with it the corpus's first position on AI.** Every prior sighting is structural: the capability is in the wrong place. 15 adds a cost curve. Making used to be expensive, so the machinery between thinking and doing was a reasonable hedge. Making is now cheap, and the machinery has not moved. Its last two lines are the claim: "The cost of finding out is falling dramatically. The cost of changing the institution around what we find out isn't." So AI does not fix M1. It widens it, because faster experiments mean "faster encounters with the bits of the institution that cannot be prototyped away."

**17 is 06 restated, nearly word for word.** 06: "Learning happens, but too late." 17: "Learning happens, but it arrives downstream of power." Ten months apart, the second now attached to Hill and Melander's "nested cultures of decision-making". It is the first time the essays quote their own best formulation back and change it. The change matters: "too late" is about timing, "downstream of power" is about position, and M1 has always been about position.

**14 runs it at team scale.** Every prior sighting places a capability somewhere in an institution and finds the decision elsewhere. 14 asks the same question inside a single team and gets the same answer: every relevant profession present, none of them with a route to the rule or the policy intent that produced the problem. It is the tightest version in the corpus, because the capability and the gap are in the same room. What it adds is the direction of travel, which is M7.

**The claim is about institutions, not about Whitehall.** This was the open question before UN80 was read: every prior instance was UK central government. The UN piece transfers the argument intact to a member-state-driven multilateral body, and names the same origin: "These dynamics are not unique to the UN. They are inherited from the 'new public management' era." It also lands the sharpest available version of M4 in one line: "The core words of UN80, impact, effectiveness, efficiency, are hallmarks of new public management."

Same *Radical How* figure, same waterfall diagnosis, same conclusion that learning arrives after commitment. The mechanism travels.

**Sub-claims that travel with it.**
- The waterfall shape puts the most consequential decisions where uncertainty is highest and learning where it is weakest (04, 06, 10, via *The Radical How*).
- Insight without authority gets absorbed rather than acted on. The "policy drawer" (06).
- Learning experienced as exception rather than condition is dismissed as "interesting but unrealistic" (06).
- **Prevention is invisible to the accountability system.** UN: "When anticipatory action averts crisis, there is no headline, ribbon-cutting moment, or clear line of attribution. The absence of failure is politically quieter than the management of visible emergencies." 04, on the same logic inside HMRC: civil servants are "punished for being wrong, rewarded for nothing going wrong, and rarely recognised when things go right." Two sightings, both sharp. Probably wants its own entry once there is a third.

**X3 is the first piece that works against M1 rather than diagnosing it.** Every row above puts a capability downstream of a decision. X3 describes a deliberate attempt to put it upstream: conditions agreed before delivery began, a standing route to the decisions sitting above the team, and small tests that give the team "something concrete to decide against, reducing how much depends on assertion or hierarchy and keeping decisions close to the people doing the work."

Not a resolution, and not yet evidence of one. It is the only account in the corpus of anyone trying, and it is written by the people trying. Read it as intent with a mechanism attached, not as a result.

**Best single formulation.** 06: "This is not a capability problem. It is an architectural one."

**What is unresolved.** See "The institutionalisation paradox" below. This is not a gap in the thinking; it is a genuine bind, and the thesis had already named it before the essays started.

**Sources.** *The Radical How* / Public Digital. Pahlka, "policy vomit". Kattel & Mazzucato on the epistemic turn. Demos Helsinki, *Missions for Governance*.

## M2. Legibility erases the thing that made it work

**The claim.** Institutions simplify a complex reality into categories that can be counted and directed. The simplification produces early gains and destroys the interdependencies the system depended on.

Four uses. The first two, 8 months apart, run the same illustration.

- **04** (Nov 25): New Public Management as a legibility drive. Scott's Prussian scientific forestry.
- **13** (Jul 26): Machinery-of-government changes as a legibility drive. Scott's Prussian scientific forestry again.
- **14** (Aug 26): professionalisation as a legibility drive. Roles became professions, professions grew capability frameworks and career paths, teams took recognisable shapes, and those shapes became "easy to recruit, grade, fund, report and hold to account". No forestry.
- **15** (Sept 26): the agile phases as a legibility drive. "An uncertain process of agile development made wonderfully legible: phases to fund, teams to organise around them, things to assure and points at which decisions could be made." Then: "somewhere along the way this map started shaping the territory." Discovery became a phase, then a deliverable, then 18 months. No forestry.

**Why this entry exists.** The forestry example is a reusable analytical device, deployed twice for different subjects, and 14 and 15 show the mechanism does not need it.

**15 turns the mechanism on the corpus's own tradition.** Every earlier subject is something the reform movement criticises: NPM, machinery-of-government churn, the professions. 15's subject is the GDS phase model, the reform movement's own method. Same generosity as 14 ("There was a logic to that separation when making was expensive"), same finding: a method built to bring thinking and making together "has matured into an architecture that can keep them remarkably far apart." Worth holding the canonical version once rather than rebuilding it each time, and worth knowing the illustration has already been spent twice so a third use is repetition.

**14 is the most generous version, which makes it the strongest.** It concedes that legibility worked: the frameworks gave digital capability "a permanence in Whitehall it would never have had otherwise". The cost is not the simplification itself but what got dropped in it, which 14 names as "the judgement, trust, permission and relationships".

**What is unresolved, and 14 is the first attempt at it.** 04 and 13 are diagnostic. 14 proposes: professions as platforms rather than silos, places to develop craft and launch from rather than boundaries that decide where the work can go, plus enabling structures instead of institutionalising ones. Borrowed from Russell, Wickson and Carew, argued about universities, untested in government. 14 also names the obvious failure: government's response to anything that works is to make it legible, so the proposition is one round of institutionalisation away from being the thing it warns about.

**Sources.** Scott, *Seeing Like a State*. Funtowicz and Ravetz, "doctrine reinforced by guesswork" (04). Russell, Wickson and Carew on platforms rather than silos (14). Dunleavy, Margetts, Bastow and Tinkler on why the professional enclaves existed in the first place (14).

## M3. The conditions are the causal thing, not the container

**The claim.** Organisational form (lab, unit, NewCo, department, studio) explains very little. What explains outcomes is the conditions around the form: protected authority, proximity to decisions, permission to experiment, evidence generated by building rather than reporting.

| Piece | How it appears |
|---|---|
| 06 | The overlaying architecture works "through roles, practices and decision rights, not new hierarchies" |
| 09 | The studio is a "place", a sociomaterial infrastructure, not a team |
| 11 | "the lab itself was never really the innovation. The innovation was the institutional conditions" |
| 13 | "the container itself tells us very little about whether that authority is real" |
| UN | "differentiated governance modes that can be activated to address systemic risks and deactivated when conditions stabilize" |
| 14 | The same professions can stay exactly where they are; "what cannot stay fixed is the architecture between them" |
| X3 | Three conditions named from inside a live NewCo: real capacity, organisational knowledge held in the team, working in the open with known routes to authority |

**UN is 06's architecture restated.** "Conditional, not universal", "triggered by uncertainty, not default", "time-bound and reversible" (06) and "activated... and deactivated when conditions stabilize" (UN) are the same design, five months apart, applied to Whitehall and then to the UN. The clearest evidence in the corpus that this is a portable claim rather than a local observation.

**Note the convergence.** 11 and 13 are the same argument applied to a lab and to a department. 13's "container" is 11's "lab, unit, NewCo, programme". These may be one entry or two; currently reads as one.

**14 is the strongest form of the claim.** Prior sightings compare containers: lab against unit, department against NewCo. 14 holds the container completely fixed. Same professions, same people, same team, and the argument is that only the architecture between them has to move. If M3 is right, that should be enough. Nothing in the corpus tests it.

**X3 is the first operationalisation, and it closes two of the three questions.** Six sightings named conditions and none said how one is made. X3 gives each of its three a mechanism.

- *Real capacity:* roles backfilled so seconded staff leave their old jobs rather than hold two. "Backfilling sounds like a resourcing detail, but it changes the relationship people have with the work."
- *Organisational knowledge:* the people who know why the processes exist sit inside the team instead of being interviewed by it.
- *Authority:* working in the open as the default, with a named escalation route held in reserve.

It also answers who grants them, which 11 asked and nothing answered: "Before delivery began, senior stakeholders across DVLA and GDS agreed a commitment to a shared ambition for the transformation work and to protecting the conditions the team would need to pursue it." Agreed up front, by named sponsors, as a precondition of starting. That is the corpus's first account of conditions being manufactured rather than observed.

**What is unresolved.** The third question survives intact. What makes conditions last through a change of sponsor is still unanswered, and X3 is the sharpest possible statement of the dependency rather than a way out of it: every condition it describes traces back to a commitment particular leaders made in advance. So the position has moved from "conditions are never operationalised" to "conditions have been operationalised once, wholly sponsor-dependent, by a team that says it is still early". 14's closing provocation, "who in government is responsible for creating the conditions that let that architecture move?", now has an answer in one case: the sponsors, in advance, in writing. Whether that is a repeatable mechanism or one well-run partnership is `prompts/01-institutionalisation-paradox.md`.

**17 proposes a second source of conditions, and it is the first that does not run through a sponsor.** X3's conditions are granted from above before the work starts. 17's are left behind by the work: relationships between policy, technical, operational and frontline colleagues, a capability another team can reuse, a governance route others can inherit, and confidence. "Together, these relationships and arrangements can begin to change the conditions under which the next experiment happens." If that holds, conditions accumulate in the organisation rather than sitting in a commitment that leaves with the person who made it, which is the only answer the corpus has to the sponsor question. It is untested, and 17 names the test: "whether the next team can pick up where we left off, rather than having to find its own way through the same treacle." Nobody has run it yet. The first team is still the only team.

## M4. Inherited logic, invisible because it lives in the framework

**The claim.** Institutions carry an operating logic from the context that produced them. No one chose it, it is not in anyone's intention, and it is invisible because it sits in the framework rather than in the decisions. Interrogating the framework is therefore the intervention.

| Piece | The framework | The inherited logic |
|---|---|---|
| 04, 06 | Policy appraisal, the Green Book | New Public Management, prediction and control |
| 08 | Western industrial policy | Market failure theory, intervene then withdraw |
| 10 | The DDaT capability framework | Commercial service design DNA from GDS 2011, "never fully been interrogated" |
| 13 | Blue tape | An interpretation that became a rule and lost its relationship to the concern that produced it |
| UN | UN80's own vocabulary | "impact, effectiveness, efficiency" as NPM hallmarks, inside a reform programme |
| 14 | "Multidisciplinary" as a team shape | A word that only means specialists working alongside one another, carrying a philosophy of empowered working it never contained |
| 15 | Discovery, alpha, beta, live | A separation that made sense "when making was expensive", kept after making got cheap |
| 17 | Letters served in writing | A legal requirement on letters going out, read as a requirement on replies coming back. "Because, well, that's what DVLA had always done" |

**17 is the first worked instance of blue tape.** 13 had the concept and no case. 17 has the case: legislation required certain letters to be served in writing, which "didn't necessarily mean the replies had to be on paper too. Yet we'd been assuming they did." An interpretation that became a rule, found by trying to change the service and not by reviewing the rule. 15 names the same category in a list, "the interpretation of a policy that has hardened into a rule", without the instance. Getting permission to test the reading still took "weeks of privacy, policy and legal conversations".

**14 generalises 10.** 10 interrogated one capability framework. 14 interrogates professionalisation itself, and adds the origin that 10 lacks: technical specialists were historically kept in professional enclaves away from the top of the civil service, and UK IT occupations only reached parity as an occupational group in 2004-5, seven years before GDS. So the frameworks solved a real exclusion before they became a constraint. That is the most sympathetic version of M4 in the corpus, and the hardest to dismiss.

**Why 13 belongs here.** Blue tape is this mechanism at the scale of a single approval process, which makes it the most testable version of the claim. 10 is the most fully argued version.

**X3 qualifies the mechanism, and it is the first pushback in the corpus.** M4 has always carried an implication that interrogating the framework releases something: the logic is inherited, nobody chose it, so surfacing it is the intervention. 13's blue tape is the purest form, an interpretation that became a rule and lost its relationship to the concern that produced it. X3, from inside a live attempt to move one: "Sometimes the team discovers that a constraint is protecting something important." And: "Understanding why a constraint exists, what it is protecting and how fixed it really is can tell us as much as successfully removing it."

Inherited logic and live protection look identical from outside. The diagnostic move M4 recommends is the same for both; the conclusion is not. The mechanism is unchanged and removal stops being its implied endpoint. Worth carrying into anything that reuses 13, because blue tape as written invites the assumption that the rule is always degraded.

**15 and 17 repeat the qualification in his own name, so it is no longer only in-role caution.** 15: "None of those things mean the governance is necessarily wrong. Scaling something into a live public service should require different assurances." 17 says it at length and with a source. Some friction "can protect important principles of public administration, while other responses can grow around precedent, perceived risk or assumptions that have become difficult to question. The difficulty is that these can look remarkably similar when you're trying to make something happen." Braams et al. supply the reason: reluctance can come from "deeply embedded understandings of what constitutes legitimate public administration". Three sightings now, two outside the institutional genre. This is the position, not a hedge. See "Constraint" under positions that have moved.

**Strongest evidence in the archive.** 10's DDaT reading: "a method of designing end-to-end journeys of a service to help users complete their goals and deliver policy intent." Then: "Deliver. Not shape. Not challenge. Not co-produce. Deliver."

## M5. Relational infrastructure is structurally underfunded

**The claim.** Public systems designed around managing needs and delivering transactions systematically underinvest in relationships, despite relationships being where change is sustained. The intermediary layer that holds relationships, integrates fragmented systems and carries learning over time is missing.

Appears in 05, 06, 07, 09, 12, 14, X3, 15, 17. The most consistent theme after M1, and the least resolved.

- **05:** social infrastructure institutions, the missing middle. Citizen Hub, Monash living labs.
- **06:** "dark matter", Dan Hill's "soft eyes", relational practices marginal to where decisions are made.
- **07:** relational welfare, pace of trust, generosity as a structural move rather than softness.
- **09:** place as sociomaterial infrastructure, Sam Rye on prototypes as devices for reorganising relationships.
- **12:** the people who run the service know the workarounds because they built them.
- **14:** the bridging between disciplines is done by individuals. "Most of this movement has happened because individuals have made it happen, which is effort rather than architecture", and "if the same people keep bridging the gaps, those bridges have to become part of the model."
- **X3:** organisational knowledge held inside the team rather than consulted, paid for by backfilling the roles people leave. Second sighting of 12's claim that the people who run the service know why it works as it does, now with a budget line attached.
- **15:** "the manual workaround quietly holding everything together". Colleagues sending every Notify message by hand is how the team found out customers had not given phone numbers. Dan Hill's "soft eyes" and dark matter again, as in 06.
- **17:** relationships as the main thing an experiment leaves behind. Sam Rye on experimentation as reorganising relationships. "Much of it sits between people... If it's going to outlast them, it has to be built into the organisation somewhere." Also the missing middle from 05, reused for the civil service: "a missing middle between ambition and practice", which experiments might "build from the inside".

**X3 answers 14's complaint directly, and it is the first time anything does.** 14 says the bridging is effort rather than architecture, and that if the same people keep bridging the gaps those bridges have to become part of the model. X3 describes a funding decision doing exactly that: backfilled roles, so holding the relationship is someone's job rather than their goodwill. M5 says relational infrastructure is structurally underfunded. This is the corpus's only instance of it being funded on purpose.

**Tension worth noticing, and the first move on it.** M1 says power sits at decision gates. M5 says change is sustained through relationships. The archive has not reconciled these. 06 gestures at it via "dark matter", 13 gets close with "which relationships help them move across formal boundaries", and 14 goes furthest with *sideways legibility*: making the architecture visible to the people trying to move through the work rather than only to the people managing it from above. That makes relationships the route to authority rather than the alternative to it. One paragraph, one sighting, own coinage. Watch it.

**17 makes the second move, and gives it a chain.** "A relationship with policy colleagues can make a different interpretation possible. That interpretation can open up a different route through governance, which in turn shapes whether a capability developed through one experiment can be used more widely." Relationship, interpretation, governance route, capability. That is the first account in the corpus of a relationship producing a change in the formal architecture, step by step, and the digital-return case is the instance behind it. It does not use 14's term and it is not the same claim: 14 is about making the architecture visible, 17 is about relationships moving it. Read together they are the nearest the corpus has come to reconciling M1 and M5. Still a sketch, and still one case.

**T is the empirical base for this entry and is not cited as such anywhere.** The thesis identifies *relational stewardship* as one of nine routines, with "moving at the speed of trust" as a direct participant quote (Participant N). Essay 07 uses "the pace of trust" and "the messy middle" without attributing either to his own fieldwork. Both phrases come from T's interviews.

**Sources.** Cottam, *Revolution 5.0* and relational welfare. Dark Matter Labs. Sam Rye. Human Learning Systems. T: Ehnert, Kivimaa, Kanda, Sovacool, Von Wirth on transition intermediaries.

## M6. Portfolio logic as the organising principle

**The claim.** Under uncertainty the unit of management is the portfolio, not the programme. Some interventions underperform, others prove catalytic, and the objective is directional progress across the system rather than uniform delivery. Portfolio logic distributes risk instead of concentrating it, which is what allows a funder to tolerate individual failures.

Three sightings, argued once.

- **04** (Nov 25), in passing: "learning at portfolio scale rather than programme-by-programme".
- **T** (Sept 25), from fieldwork: "aligning portfolios of initiatives" as a routine of dynamic stewardship.
- **UN** (Jun 26), argued properly: portfolio management is the organising principle that makes the other three shifts cohere. Leuven 2030 as the worked case.

**Why this is now an entry rather than a watch item.** It is the only proposal in the corpus that answers "how does funding follow evidence in practice", which M1 needs and does not otherwise have. UN: "funding should follow evidence, not the other way around", with quarterly reallocation across a climate-displacement portfolio as the worked example. Compare 11 on early GDS: "Governance, funding, resources all followed the evidence. Not the other way round." Same claim, one from institutional history, one as a design proposal.

**Sources.** Leuven 2030 (also cited in 02). Vinnova. Mazzucato.

## M7. The work moves vertically, and there is no route through the layers

**The claim.** Work does not stay at the altitude where it appears. A service problem turns out to be an operational rule, the rule encodes a judgement about risk, and the judgement was made against a policy intent nobody in the delivery team wrote. Moving between those layers is a different capability from moving between disciplines, and it belongs to no profession, no team and no role.

Three sightings, three evidence bases, one object.

| Piece | Date | How it arrives |
|---|---|---|
| T | Sept 25 | Named as a routine: "shifting altitudes", moving between the "meta" and the "matter", translating lived experience upwards and systemic shifts downwards |
| 13 | Jul 26 | Unnamed: "Direction and public purpose travel down. Evidence about what helps or obstructs delivery travels back up" |
| 14 | Aug 26 | Argued: work moves horizontally across kinds of expertise and vertically through layers of institutional authority, and a team can be complete on the first while having no route through the second |
| X3 | Sept 26 | Given a route and a name: "air cover", a standing line to senior sponsorship for decisions sitting above the team |
| 15 | Sept 26 | Met at the point of scale: "technology capacity owned somewhere else, an assurance route proportionate to what you're trying to do, or a decision that nobody in the team actually has the right to make" |
| 17 | Oct 26 | Drawn: the navigation cone, five layers from citizen interaction to political and strategic support, each moving at its own pace. "Suddenly, returning a form digitally isn't really a question about the form at all" |

**17 adds time to the layers.** Every earlier sighting treats the layers as levels of authority. 17 treats them as speeds too, via Brand's pace layers: an interaction changes in an afternoon, the process behind it in months, priorities and funding in budget years, institutional commitments over parliaments. "Fast gets all our attention, slow has all the power." So the vertical move is not only up a hierarchy, it is into something that changes more slowly than the experiment does. 17 also refuses to treat the slowness as the enemy: "Some of it is what allows an institution to maintain its responsibilities while everything else changes around it."

**17 also offers a second route, and it is not escalation.** X3's route is air cover, a line upward to be used sparingly. 17's is proximity: you cannot answer the slower layers' questions yourself (who owns this, what would it cost, what is the governance), "but you can bring the people who might answer them close enough to see the experiment happen." One brings the problem to authority, the other brings authority to the work. X3 prices the first. Nothing yet prices the second, which probably costs senior attention and is limited by it.

**Why this is an entry now rather than a watch item.** It sat on the watch list on two sightings, one of them unnamed. 14 argues it at length and separates it cleanly from the horizontal movement that the cross-disciplinary literature already covers, which is what the other two lacked. It also supplies the chain: interaction, process, rule, judgement, policy intent.

**Relationship to M1.** This is M1 from inside the team. M1 says the capability sits downstream of the decision. M7 says what that is like for the people holding it: they can see where the work needs to go and have no way to get there. M1 is the diagnosis, M7 is the movement the diagnosis blocks.

**X3 supplies a route and a price.** "When something does need authority elsewhere, the partnership gives us a route to senior sponsorship. We call this 'air cover'. Leaders have already committed to the conditions around the work, so escalation is about unblocking rather than re-stating the case." First named vertical route in the corpus, and the first that exists before it is needed rather than being improvised at the point of blockage.

It also prices it, which no prior sighting does: "Escalating too quickly moves decisions away from the people closest to the work and turns senior sponsorship into part of the everyday operating model." The route degrades through use. Air cover spent on ordinary decisions stops being air cover and becomes a hierarchy. That cuts against the corpus's own instinct, which has been to treat access to authority as the scarce good and therefore more of it as better.

**What is unresolved.** X3 answers what the route is and what it costs, for one team. It does not say who is permitted to use it, how the judgement about when to escalate actually gets made, or what happens to the route when the sponsors who granted it move on. T's routine is still descriptive, observed in intermediaries doing it without sanction. 17 follows one case up through the layers and stops where the move would have to be completed: making the digital route last "would mean the capability being prioritised, paid for and owned", and the essay does not say whether it was. So the corpus now has a vertical move traced as far as the authority, and still none completed with the authority trail attached. Nothing says how anyone gets good at the judgement either. See `prompts/06-depth-that-travels.md` and `prompts/07-the-missing-phase.md`.

**Sources.** T, on shifting altitudes as a routine of dynamic stewardship. Wright Morton, Eigenbrode and Martin on architectures of adaptive integration (14). Brand on pace layers, and Hill and Melander on nested cultures of decision-making (17).

## M8. The experiment is a probe into the institution

**The claim.** Putting something real into a live service, however small, shows what the institution is made of in a way that studying it cannot. The experiment runs into the system that does not connect, the data that is not there, the interpretation that has hardened into a rule. How the organisation responds tells you as much about its capacity to change as the result tells you about the idea. So the experiment's main output is knowledge about the institution, and the service change is the occasion for it.

Promoted from the watch list ("test and learn as organisational self-knowledge") on 15, which argues it, and 17, which is built on it.

| Piece | Date | How it arrives |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | Jun 26 | "Test the organisation, not just the service." Can the organisation tolerate the uncertainty learning requires |
| Note | Aug 26 | What released capacity makes possible: roles, operating models, decision rights, risk (Substack note, not an article) |
| X3 | Sept 26 | In passing: "we should learn the operating model through delivery". The assurance route as the instance |
| 15 | Sept 26 | Argued: "The thing you make becomes a probe into the institution around it." "We had to build the thing to find the skeleton" |
| 17 | Oct 26 | The whole essay. Experiments as probes, then as navigation, architecture and stewardship. Dan Hill's "dark matter probes", via Bryan Boyer |

**Two worked cases, both DVLA Drivers Medical.**
- *Notify (15).* A test of proactive texts. The message worked. Notify was not connected to the caseworking system, so colleagues sent every text by hand, and doing that showed that many customers had never given a phone number. Two problems found, one upstream of the other, and neither visible from a process map.
- *Digital return (17).* 100 customers allowed to send information back digitally instead of by post. People used it. The finding was the assumption that replies had to be on paper, which turned out to be a reading of a law about letters going out (M4). Permission took weeks.

**Why it is a mechanism and not a method.** It changes what an experiment is for. In the test and learn framing the experiment tests an intervention. In this one it probes an institution, and 17 says so in its second paragraph: the digital-return test was "less to prove that letting customers return things digitally was a good idea... and more to probe the system". It also changes the value-for-money case, which 15 makes directly: time spent finding out is no longer time spent before the service improves.

**Relationship to the others.** M1 says the capability sits downstream of the decision. M8 is how you find out where the decision actually sits: push something through and see where it stops. M4 says the inherited logic is invisible because it is in the framework. M8 is how it becomes visible. And M7 is where the probe goes once it is in: up through the layers.

**15 prices it.** "Finding a skeleton doesn't necessarily mean you have the power to move it." The probe shows you the institution and gives you no authority over it. 15's AI argument makes this worse, not better: probing gets cheaper every year and moving what it finds does not. So M8 on its own produces a growing list of known problems with no owner. That is M1 again, at higher speed.

**What is unresolved.** Every case is from one partnership, written by the people running it. There is no probe that went wrong: one that cost a service something, damaged a relationship or got shut down. And there is no account of who reads the findings. 17 lists what an experiment leaves behind, but the knowledge about the institution, the main output by its own argument, has no named place to go. See `prompts/07-the-missing-phase.md`.

**Sources.** Hill, via Boyer, on dark matter probes. Hill on soft eyes. Brand, *Maintenance: Of Everything*, via Pirsig: to look after something you have to know how it works and how it was made (15). Sam Rye on experimentation as reorganising relationships (17).

## The institutionalisation paradox

Not a mechanism. The corpus's central unresolved question, and the reason M1 has nine diagnoses and no transition.

**T states it precisely, in Sept 2025, before almost all of the essays:**

> "This raises the unresolved question of institutionalisation: without it, practices risk remaining peripheral; with it, they may lose the freedom and adaptability that makes them effective in the first place."

Every later piece that reaches this wall reaches the same formulation, independently and from a different direction:

| Piece | Date | How it arrives |
|---|---|---|
| T | Sept 25 | 15 interviews, 4 countries. Intermediaries compensate for weak dynamic capabilities and are precarious without institutionalisation |
| 09 | Mar 26 | How a studio gains authority to reshape live decisions rather than inform them from a distance is "one I'm still working through" |
| 11 | May 26 | Whether a NewCo can hold protected authority as it scales, or is absorbed into the governance logic that constrained its predecessors |
| 12 | Jun 26 | "Do these protected spaces eventually replace parts of the original organisation? Do they get absorbed back into it? Or do they diffuse into nothing once the attention moves elsewhere? We are too early to know" |
| 14 | Aug 26 | From the research literature: institutionalising cross-disciplinary work produced "mega-silos", new vertical structures that replaced disciplinary boundaries with topic and industry ones |
| X3 | Sept 26 | The same live case as 12, three months on, stating conditions rather than the bind: "We are still early in this work" |
| 15 | Sept 26 | The same live case again, in his own name, at the scale of one experiment: "the very things that made the experiment possible would make a scaled version impossible" |
| 16 | Oct 26 | Stated as the publication's own open question: "What should be institutionalised, and what needs to remain flexible?" |

**X3 does not add a sixth arrival, and that is the finding.** It is 12's case three months later, and it reports conditions rather than the bind. The genre explains most of that: an institutional post cannot publish doubt about whether its own arrangement will survive. But it leaves the count at five independent arrivals, and means the case sitting closest to the question is the one least able to discuss it in public. Anything written from X3 has to get the bind from elsewhere in the corpus.

**15 is where it comes from, and it finds the bind at a smaller scale.** Not counted as a sixth independent arrival, since it is the same case as 12 and X3. What it adds is a new object. Every earlier arrival is about a protected space: a lab, a studio, a NewCo. 15's is about a single intervention. Notify could be tested because colleagues could send texts by hand, so the test borrowed what existed and skipped the technology dependencies. "You cannot scale the workaround forever." The conditions that let the experiment happen are the conditions that stop it becoming the service. Same bind, one level down, and the first time it has been shown with a case rather than a question.

**16 is the author naming it as his own question.** Not an arrival either: there is no evidence base behind it. But it is the first time the bind appears in the publication's own description of what it is for, a year in. The question has moved from something the pieces reach to something the publication is organised around.

**Why this matters for the wiki.** The question is not open because it has been under-thought. It is a genuine bind with a formulation already in hand, arrived at five times from five different evidence bases. That is a much stronger position than the essays individually suggest, and none of them says so, because none of them can see the other four at once.

**14 is the first piece to take a side.** The other four state the bind and stop. 14 argues that structures built to institutionalise this kind of working are unlikely to succeed and are hostile to the flexibility it depends on, and that what is wanted instead is enabling structures: opportunities for interaction, groupings driven by the people doing the work, incentives and quality criteria. The position is borrowed rather than derived, from Russell, Wickson and Carew writing about universities, and 14 says so.

It also supplies the corpus's first negative case with numbers attached. Australia's Cooperative Research Centres: 16 centres funded in 2004, roughly half the 2002 number and selected down from over 50 initially established, consolidated into designated research strengths chosen largely on commercial potential, with cross-disciplinary work starved in the spaces between. Prompt 01 asked for a case of institutionalisation flattening something. There is now one, from another country and another sector, and none from government.

The strongest line for reuse is 14's own section heading: transdisciplinarity is a practice, not an institution. That is a position, not a resolution, and the bind is still the bind.

**T's own answer, such as it is.** "The challenge is less about treating intermediaries as fillers of capability gaps, and more about exploring how their practices can be stabilised and resourced without neutralising their generative potential." That is a restatement of the bind rather than a way out, and the thesis says so.

**The nearest thing to a resolution in the corpus** is 11 on early GDS: authority, learning and delivery stayed connected "long enough for evidence to accumulate", and legitimacy came from working software rather than reports. 09 makes the same bet explicitly: "legitimacy in this space is built through action, not insight." Both propose that the paradox is dissolved by sequencing, not by design. Untested.

**17 is a third version of the sequencing bet, and the first with a mechanism.** 11 and 09 say legitimacy comes from action. 17 says what the action leaves behind: relationships, a reusable capability, a governance route, confidence, each one making the next experiment easier. Nothing gets institutionalised as a unit; the organisation is changed one experiment at a time until the protected space is less needed. That sidesteps the bind rather than resolving it, because nothing is ever formally absorbed. It is also exactly the "diffuse into nothing" outcome 12 feared, if the residue does not stick. 17's own test separates the two: whether the next team can pick up where the last one left off. Not yet run.

## Capability schema: settled

Two schemas existed. The working one is **sense-making, connecting, shaping**, from *Beyond the waterfall state* (06) via IIPP.

The thesis used four dimensions and nine routines, mapped to sensing, seizing and transforming. That was a point in time and is superseded. It stays in the corpus as evidence, not as vocabulary: its fieldwork is still the empirical base for M5 and its formulation of the institutionalisation paradox is still the best one anyone has written. The schema is retired.

Anything drafted from here uses the three.

## Positions that have moved

The archive's most valuable artefacts, because a stream of essays cannot hold both ends of a revision and this file can.

**Public design: 02 → 10.** Seven months.
- 02 (Sept 25): design is "a malleable practice", holds systems lightly, "not a solution in itself, but it is a start". Optimistic, conference-shaped.
- 10 (Apr 26): "I'm not convinced." The profession's structures, not its people, are the constraint. "You can't iterate your way to transformation using a framework optimised for delivery."
- What changed: from design as promising practice to design as a professionalisation problem.

**Innovation labs: 06 → 11.** Four months, and a genuine revision rather than a deepening.
- 06 (Jan 26): labs appear as example 1 of innovation collapsing back into business as usual.
- 11 (May 26): "The first wave genuinely worked." MindLab, Helsinki Design Lab, early GDS produced rigorous work. The conditions disappeared afterwards.
- What changed: from labs as a structural failure to labs as a structural success whose enabling conditions were not sustained. 11 is more historically generous and more precise.

**Multidisciplinary teams: 06, 11, 12 → 14.** Seven months, and the revision is aimed at the corpus's own shorthand.
- 06 (Jan 26), 11 (May 26), 12 (Jun 26): "a small, multidisciplinary team" with political cover, protected space and authority to bypass routine constraints, used as shorthand for the entire set of conditions. 11 applies it to CustomerFirst, 12 writes it from inside.
- 14 (Aug 26): the word carries none of that. It says the professions are present, and nothing about whether knowledge is integrated, whether authority can move, whether the team can cross an organisational boundary, or whether its shape can change when the problem does.
- What changed: the shorthand got audited. The conditions are still the claim, which is M3. The word is no longer trusted to carry them.

**Transdisciplinary: 01 → 14.** Eleven months, and the corpus doubling back on its first word.
- 01 (Sept 25) describes CIVICWORKS as "intentionally transdisciplinary", meaning drawing on as many perspectives as possible. A mood.
- 14 gives it the technical sense from the research literature: goals formed in common, knowledge produced together, disciplinary and sectoral boundaries crossed rather than handed across. A structure.
- What changed: nothing was retracted, which is the point. The publication used a precise term loosely for a year before arriving at what it means, and 14 claims government has never had a word for the thing. Worth knowing before the word is used loosely again.
- 16 (Oct 26), the one-year re-introduction, drops the word. 01 said "intentionally transdisciplinary". 16 says "intentionally bouncing around different disciplines". It was not used loosely again.

**Constraint: 13 → X3 → 15, 17.** Three months, and the clearest case of the essays absorbing something first said in role.
- 13 (Jul 26): blue tape, an interpretation that became a rule and lost its link to the concern behind it. The bureaucracy we can cut is something you can identify and remove.
- X3 (Sept 26), in role: "Sometimes the team discovers that a constraint is protecting something important."
- 15 and 17 (Sept, Oct 26), in his own name: the governance is not necessarily wrong, friction can protect principles of public administration, and the protective kind and the habitual kind "can look remarkably similar when you're trying to make something happen". 17 adds Braams et al. on why reluctance is often reasonable.
- What changed: from sorting bureaucracy into kept and cut, to not being able to tell which is which until you push on it. 13's distinction survives. What has gone is the idea that you can apply it from outside. The probe (M8) is now how you tell.

**Protected space: 11 → 12.** One month, diagnosis to live case.
- 11 asks whether a NewCo can hold protected authority as it scales.
- 12 reports from inside it and refuses to answer: "We are too early to know which of these is happening to us."

## What the archive does not have

Honest gaps, which are as useful as the entries.

- **No failure case written up in detail.** The argument is consistently that the architecture defeats good work. No piece follows a single decision through the architecture to show it happening. T is the closest, and its cases are intermediaries succeeding precariously rather than a decision going wrong. 14 traces the chain in the abstract — interaction, rule, evidence requirement, risk judgement, policy intent — with no case attached. That abstraction is the exact shape of the piece that is missing. X3 gets closest to naming the occasion, "an experiment may run into a technology constraint that has been treated as fixed for years", and supplies no instance of one.

  15 and 17 narrow it. They are the first pieces to follow a single change into the institution with a case: Notify in 15, digital return in 17. Both stop where the decision would have been. 15 stops at "a decision that nobody in the team actually has the right to make", 17 at the capability needing to be "prioritised, paid for and owned". The corpus now has the chain with a case attached, and still no ending: nothing on whether the decision was made, by whom, or what it took.

- **The thesis is barely cited by the writing that depends on it.** 02 and 04 promise it as forthcoming. 05 covers the same territory via Recurve. 07 uses two of its participant phrases. 17 is the first piece to cite it, in a reference list, without drawing on its findings. It is the corpus's largest single piece of primary evidence, 15 interviews across 4 countries, and the newsletter still does not use it.
- **M1 has no proposed transition, and now there is a reason.** See the institutionalisation paradox. The corpus has five independent arrivals at the same bind and one untested bet on sequencing. X3 is the first described attempt at a transition, in one case, reported by the people attempting it.

- **Nothing on conditions being withdrawn.** M3 now has an account of conditions being created, in X3, and none of what happens when a sponsor leaves, a partnership ends or a commitment made before delivery stops being honoured. The corpus can say how protected space gets built and still cannot say how it ends. Every live-case piece stops at the same point, because the case has not got there yet.
- **M5 and M1 are not reconciled.** Power at decision gates versus change through relationships. 14 makes the first move with sideways legibility, one paragraph. 17 makes the second, a chain from relationship to interpretation to governance route, with one case behind it. Closer, still not done.

- **No account of what the professions lose.** 14 argues professional boundaries should be permeable, then explicitly parks the politics. Digital specialists spent 20 years getting professional standing, and only reached occupational-group parity in 2004-5. The corpus now has an argument that runs against something its own readers fought for, and nothing on who carries the cost. See `prompts/05-politics-of-professionalisation.md`.
- **Almost no citizen-side evidence outside 07 and 12.** The archive argues about institutions from inside institutions. 15 and 17 add two facts from the citizen side, the missing phone numbers and 100 people using the digital route, and both serve the argument about the institution.

- **A position on AI, and only one.** Until September there were two asides, 06 and 13. 15 takes a position: AI makes finding out cheap and leaves changing the institution as expensive as before, so it makes M1 worse. 16 restates it as an open question. That is a position about the speed of experiments. It says nothing about what AI does to the work itself, the roles or the released capacity, which is what most public sector AI is being scoped around. See `prompts/04-released-capacity.md`.

- **The live-case evidence is one partnership.** 12, the CustomerFirst material in 14, X3, 15 and 17 are all CustomerFirst, and 15 and 17 are both DVLA Drivers Medical. M8 rests almost entirely on it, and M3, M5, M7 and the paradox have their most recent evidence there. That is not a criticism of the case, which nobody else in the corpus could have written. It is the reason the next piece needs evidence from somewhere else, even second-hand.
