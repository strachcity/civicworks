# What released capacity is for

**Status:** claim in hand, evidence almost absent. Comes from a Substack note ([Aug 2026](https://substack.com/@jackstrachan/note/c-321460859)) rather than from the archive. The corpus has two passing mentions of AI across 13 pieces and no position.

## The claim

AI transformation is being scoped against the organisation that already exists: where can a process be automated, time saved, demand reduced. The more consequential question is what the released capacity makes possible. Which roles become more important. Where people spend more time on judgement, relationships and complexity. Where value created in one part of government shows up somewhere else entirely. AI might change a service. The transformation is everything that gets changed around it.

## Why it can be written now

**It is 12's third question at a larger scale.** 12: "what does that unlock for the wider transformation?" and "the harder transformation decisions that come later — the ones involving operating models, risk tolerance, policy constraints and organisational boundaries". The note names almost the same list: future roles, operating models, decision rights, risk and capabilities. That is the second sighting of the watch-list item, and the first one pointed forwards rather than inwards.

**It is M1 with a new occasion.** Automation decisions get made up front, in a business case or an efficiency target, and what the organisation learns about itself arrives afterwards. The note does not make this argument. It is the obvious one to make and it is the reason the piece belongs to this corpus rather than to the AI commentary pile.

**The sequencing bet, again.** Use live service changes to test future roles, operating models and decision rights. Same wager as 09, 11 and 12: legitimacy through action, not insight. Still untested, now with a fourth arrival.

**The time horizon is the one new move.** Services being changed today will exist in some form in 100 years, so the organisation that delivers them is a legitimate object of design. Nothing else in the corpus argues from duration. It is also the weakest link: 100 years is rhetoric until a decision depends on it.

## What is missing

**A case where capacity was released and something was done with it.** UK, public sector, with the redeployment visible. Without it this is a position rather than an argument.

**Whether the capacity is ever actually released.** Efficiency savings in government are usually banked as headcount reduction, not redeployed into judgement work. The piece has to meet that directly or it is describing a choice nobody is offered.

**The IKEA story** now circulating as a graphic: 8,500 customer service roles automated, everyone retrained as interior designers, $1.4bn of new revenue. Not usable as it stands. No source on the card, private sector retail, and "every human kept their jobs" is the shape second-hand claims take on the way round. If it is used at all, quote Ingka's own reporting, check what the figures actually cover, and say what the transition cost.

## What would make it fail

**Becoming an AI piece.** AI is the occasion. The subject is what an organisation is for. 08 failed this way with fintech.

**Optimism without a mechanism.** "People freed up for higher-value work" is the promise New Public Management made about efficiency, and M4 says the intervention is interrogating the framework. "Released capacity" is a framework. It should be interrogated before anything is built on top of it.
