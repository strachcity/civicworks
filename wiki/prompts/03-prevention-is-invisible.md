# Prevention is invisible to the accountability system

**Status:** two sightings, both sharp, neither argued at length. Needs a third instance or a case before it is worth drafting.

## The claim

Institutions cannot see prevention, because its success is the absence of an event. What follows is not that officials undervalue prevention but that no accountability framework in use can register it, so stewardship and foresight are structurally unrewarded whatever anyone believes about their importance.

## Why it is on the list

It is the only causal explanation in the corpus for *why* M1 persists. Every other piece describes capability sitting downstream. This says what keeps it there: the incentive gradient points at visible activity, and learning that prevents a failure produces nothing to report.

## Evidence in hand

- **UN80:** "When anticipatory action averts crisis, there is no headline, ribbon-cutting moment, or clear line of attribution. The absence of failure is politically quieter than the management of visible emergencies."
- **04**, the same logic inside HMRC: civil servants are "punished for being wrong, rewarded for nothing going wrong, and rarely recognised when things go right."

UN80 also proposes the fix: assess whether teams identified emerging risks early, whether partnerships improved collective response, whether interventions altered the trajectory of a known risk. Proposed, not tested.

## What is missing

A worked case. Ideally a real decision where prevention lost to visible activity, with the accountability trail showing why. 13's DSIT material is adjacent (reorganisation is legible, the conditions for good work are not) and could be the way in.

## What would make it fail

Becoming a piece about performance management. The interesting claim is epistemic: the system cannot see the thing, not that it measures it badly.
