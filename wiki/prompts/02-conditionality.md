# Who carries the cost, who captures the return

**Status:** ready, and partly a rewrite. Piece 08 made this argument in about 900 words and converted no one.

## The claim

Public investment routinely absorbs early uncertainty while private actors capture scale-driven returns, and the cause is not the size of the investment but the design of the partnership. Conditionality is a design object. It is almost never designed.

## Why it can be written now

08 has the argument and no room. Its own line: "The architecture of partnership, rather than the volume of investment, becomes the central design flaw." Then it stops.

It is also the clearest instance in the corpus of a mechanism outside M1, which matters: five of six mechanisms are about decision architecture, and a publication needs more than one spine.

## Evidence in hand

The NICE and NHS England antimicrobial subscription model, where revenue is delinked from volume and "the whole mechanism was redesigned, not just the funding level". Korea's MyData and open banking rails, 39 million users, as market-shaping in practice. David Eaves on which layers of the stack a country chooses to commodify and govern. Market failure theory as the inherited logic, which connects it to M4.

Portfolio logic (M6) is the other half and 08 predates it: the UN80 piece argues that funding should follow evidence with quarterly reallocation. Conditionality decides who captures the return, portfolio logic decides how the bets are held. They have never been argued together.

## What is missing

A UK case where conditionality was designed and failed, or was absent and cost something measurable. The antimicrobial model is the good example; the piece needs the counterpart. Without it this is a single success story and a foreign comparison.

## What would make it fail

Writing it as a fintech piece. Fintech is the occasion. The subject is what the state gets back for carrying risk.

Reheating 08. The Korea event framing was what capped it at 900 words in the first place.
