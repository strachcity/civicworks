# Depth that travels

**Status:** claim in hand, evidence is one live case and one paper. The weakest prompt on the list, here because 14 named it as unfinished and because M7 has no answer without it.

## The claim

Moving sideways across disciplines and upwards through institutional layers is a capability, not a personality. It is learned, it depends on having depth to travel from, and in the one live case the corpus has it is being done by individuals as effort rather than held as architecture. The failure mode is generalism, which is what crossing boundaries looks like when someone does not know where their own expertise ends.

## Why it can be written now

**M7 needs it.** The corpus now has three sightings of work moving vertically through layers of authority and nothing at all on who is able to do that or how they got good at it.

**14 has the live observation and does not develop it.** CustomerFirst started with a deliberately loose structure and the workstreams drifted back into technology, data, governance and organisation design silos. What pushed against that drift was individuals: "Most of this movement has happened because individuals have made it happen, which is effort rather than architecture."

**The theoretical claim is already cited.** Nowotny (2003), that expertise increasingly involves building links between what experts know and what other people need to know and do. 14 uses it once, in a sentence, and moves on.

**The boundary condition is already stated.** 14: people "step into unfamiliar areas when the work needs it" while "still knowing where their expertise ends and when somebody with deeper knowledge needs to come in." That is the testable version, and nothing tests it.

## Evidence in hand

CustomerFirst, live and current, which is the only real base. Nowotny on socially robust knowledge. T's fieldwork on intermediaries performing shifting altitudes without sanction. 12's observation that the people who run the service know the workarounds because they built them. 14's claim that the bridges individuals build have to become part of the model.

## What is missing

**How anyone learns it.** Whether it can be taught or only accumulated, and what the accumulation actually is: tenure, exposure, having worked in more than one function, having been junior somewhere unfamiliar.

**Observable criteria.** What distinguishes someone who crosses well from a generalist, stated so that a third party could tell them apart. Without this the piece is a description of people the author already rates.

**Whether the CustomerFirst bridgers share anything.** Background, seniority, tenure, discipline. If the only common factor is willingness, that is a finding and it undermines the capability claim.

**Any evidence outside one programme.** T is the nearest and its intermediaries sit outside institutions rather than inside a department.

## What would make it fail

**T-shaped people.** The vocabulary exists, it is thin, and reaching for it turns this into a capability-framework piece. M4 says the framework is where the inherited logic lives, and prompt 05 says what interrogating this one costs.

**Writing it from CustomerFirst alone.** The programme is months old and 12 already published the honest answer for this stage: we are too early to know. Two prompts now depend on that case, which is a reason to be careful with it, not a reason to lean harder.

**Turning it into advice.** "Be curious, stay humble, learn about other disciplines" is the shape this collapses into. If the piece cannot name a structural condition that makes the movement more likely, it has become a LinkedIn post.
