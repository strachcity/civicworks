# The missing phase

**Status:** claim in hand, two cases, both from one partnership. 15 states the claim in a paragraph and moves on. 17 sketches half an answer without connecting it back. Nobody has written the piece that joins them.

## The claim

The agile phase model has no phase for changing the institution. Discovery, alpha, beta and live are phases for building a service, and beta assumes that the technology ownership, the assurance route and the authority to decide already exist. Test and learn has made finding out cheap, so teams now reach that gap faster and more often, with something worth keeping and nowhere to take it. What "scaling what works" needs to scale is not the intervention. It is the change to the institution that the intervention exposed.

## Why it can be written now

**15 has the claim, in one paragraph.** "Discovery, alpha, beta and live are phases for building a service. There is no phase for changing who owns the technology, who assures it or who gets to decide. Beta quietly assumes all of that already exists, and the team discovers otherwise at exactly the moment it has something worth keeping." Then: "This is the gap the institutionalisation of digital delivery never built a home for." That is a thesis, and it got 80 words.

**15 also has the case.** Notify was testable because colleagues sent texts by hand. "You cannot scale the workaround forever. Suddenly you need technology capacity owned somewhere else, an assurance route proportionate to what you're trying to do, or a decision that nobody in the team actually has the right to make." Plus Bradach and Grindle on how unclear "scale" has always been: "what exactly are we scaling?"

**17 has half an answer and does not notice it.** Its architecture section lists what an experiment leaves in the organisation: relationships, a capability other teams can reuse (the digital return route), a governance route others can inherit, confidence. That is a description of the missing phase's outputs. 17 never connects it to 15's gap. And its stewardship section supplies the reason the phase cannot be skipped: some of what blocks scale is protecting something, and you cannot tell which until you push.

**It is the mechanisms meeting at one point.** M8, the experiment as probe, says what you find. M1 says the authority sits elsewhere. M7 says it sits up through layers that move more slowly. M4 says some of what you find is inherited and some is protective. The missing phase is where all four arrive at once, which is why a team feels it as a single wall.

## Evidence in hand

15's Notify case and its phase-model argument. 17's digital return case, traced to the point where the capability needed to be "prioritised, paid for and owned", and its list of what experiments leave behind. X3's air cover as one route across the gap, and its price. Bradach and Grindle on scale. The GDS service manual, which defines the phases and says nothing about who owns what at the end of beta.

## What is missing

**A case that crossed.** Something found in a test that became how the service works, with the decision trail: who decided, what it cost, how long it took, what had to change above the team. Without one this is a complaint about a gap. With one it is a description of the phase.

**A case from outside CustomerFirst.** Both cases are DVLA Drivers Medical. Any service team in government that has taken a beta into live has met this, so the evidence exists. It just is not in the corpus.

**What the phase would contain.** Not a new gate. If the piece proposes "a fifth phase" it has proposed more machinery between thinking and doing, which 15 argues against. The useful version says what work happens there and who does it, and admits it might not look like a phase at all.

## What would make it fail

**GDS-bashing.** 15 is generous about why the phases exist, and the generosity is where its authority comes from. The phase model solved a real problem when making was expensive. The piece is about what changed, not about who got it wrong.

**Inventing a phase.** See above. The finding is the gap, not a box to fill it with.

**Restating the paradox.** Prompt 01 is about whether a protected space survives. This is about whether one finding becomes the service. They connect, since the second is the paradox at the scale of a single intervention, but if the piece drifts to labs and NewCos it has become prompt 01.

**Writing it while the case is unfinished.** If digital return has not crossed yet, the honest version says so, the way 12 said "we are too early to know". That is a better piece than one that guesses the ending.
